# 01 — Expert Intelligence: What the Big Names Actually Recommend (and What the Evidence Says)

**Scope:** Cortisol / chronic stress, visceral (belly) fat, metabolic health, longevity.
**Audience:** An adult (sex/age unknown) on a 3-month program to lower chronic stress and lose abdominal/visceral fat.
**Method:** Web research (Oct 2026) on each figure's public protocols, cross-checked against trials and reviews where available. Doses below are what these people *report using or recommending*. They are not prescriptions. Where a dose comes only from secondary summaries, it is marked "(reported)".

**Evidence grades used**
- **Strong:** consistent RCTs or meta-analyses in humans on outcomes that matter (fat loss, visceral fat, sleep, BP, glycemia).
- **Moderate:** some RCTs, but small, short, surrogate endpoints, or mixed.
- **Weak:** mechanistic, animal, anecdotal, or a single small pilot.
- **Contradicted:** human trials show no benefit, or harm.

---

## Bottom line up front

1. **Almost everyone agrees on the boring core:** sleep 7–9 h on a fixed schedule; morning outdoor light; daily walking plus Zone-2 cardio; 2–3+ resistance sessions a week; a modest calorie deficit built on protein, fiber and whole foods; cutting sugar-sweetened drinks, refined carbs and ultra-processed food; limiting alcohol; and a daily down-regulation practice (breathing, meditation, time in nature). This core has the best evidence for **both** lowering stress physiology and shrinking visceral fat.
2. **Meal timing (time-restricted eating) is a useful adherence tool, not magic.** In a 12-month NEJM RCT it produced no extra loss of visceral or liver fat beyond the same calorie deficit.
3. **Supplements are second-order.** The ones with real (moderate) evidence for stress, sleep or cardiometabolic markers are ashwagandha (with liver/thyroid caveats), magnesium, omega-3s, vitamin D *if deficient*, creatine (for training), and possibly L-theanine. None of them burn belly fat.
4. **Exclude:** coffee enemas and Gerson-style juice regimens, unsupervised rapamycin/metformin and other prescription "longevity" drugs, young-plasma or blood transfusions, MTHFR-driven supplement upselling, extended water fasts without supervision, extreme cold or heat exposure for people with cardiovascular risk, and 100-pill stacks.

## 1. David Sinclair

**Credentials:** PhD (UNSW). Professor of Genetics at Harvard Medical School and co-director of the Paul F. Glenn Center for Aging. A legitimate, highly cited aging biologist. Founder, investor or board member of dozens of companies (28 per a 2019 KFF Health News count).

**Publicly reported protocol (from *Lifespan* and interviews):**
- NMN about **1 g/day** in the morning (reported).
- Resveratrol **0.5–1 g/day**, taken with yogurt or olive oil for absorption (reported; sources disagree on the dose).
- Metformin about **800 mg–1 g at night**, skipped on exercise days (reported, prescription). He has since said he has paused it at times.
- Skips one meal a day or keeps it very small (informal intermittent fasting / 16:8-style); eats mostly plants; limits sugar.
- Exercise, sauna and cold exposure framed as "hormetic stress"; regular biomarker blood draws.

**Evidence:**
| Claim | Grade | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Resveratrol activates sirtuins and extends healthspan | **Contradicted / weak** | The Sirtris/GSK program failed to deliver. Other labs struggled to replicate the core SIRT1 activation results. Human trials show no meaningful metabolic or weight benefit. Kaeberlein calls it "a clinical bust". |
| NMN/NAD+ boosting slows aging | **Weak** | Small human RCTs show NAD levels rise. One trial (Yoshino 2021, *Science*) showed better muscle insulin sensitivity in prediabetic women. There is no evidence of visceral fat loss, cortisol reduction or longevity benefit. |
| Metformin for longevity in non-diabetics | **Weak / unproven** | The TAME trial is not complete. Metformin may blunt aerobic and strength gains from exercise (MASTERS trial). |
| Eat less, skip a meal, eat mostly plants | **Moderate–strong** (for weight) | This works through a calorie deficit. It is not sirtuin-specific. |

**Red flags:** In 2024 his company Animal Biosciences claimed a dog supplement had "reversed aging". After the backlash and a wave of resignations, he left the presidency of the Academy for Health and Lifespan Research. Critics such as Matt Kaeberlein call his claims overhyped. He has many commercial conflicts of interest. Regulatory note: the FDA reversed course in Sept 2025 and NMN is again lawful as a supplement. That ruling is about legal status, not proof that it works.

**Usable for this plan:** "Eat less, mostly plants, skip or shrink one meal, exercise." Skip NMN and resveratrol. They are expensive and have no proven effect on cortisol or belly fat.

## 2. Gary Brecka ("Breka")

**Credentials:** He calls himself a "human biologist." By his own account he spent about 20 years in life-insurance mortality modelling (predicting time of death for underwriting). He says he holds biology/human-biology degrees, but independent verification of his education is poor and online bios contradict each other. Claims that he trained in mortuary science circulate but I could not verify them. **He is not a physician, PhD, registered dietitian or licensed clinician.** He co-founded 10X Health System (genetic testing plus supplements) and hosts *The Ultimate Human* podcast.

**Core protocol (as he publicizes it):**
- "Three no-cost hacks": **morning sunlight** (barefoot, early), **grounding/earthing** (bare feet on earth), and **breathwork** (Wim-Hof-style cycles of rapid breathing plus breath holds).
- **Cold plunge** (daily, a few minutes).
- **Methylated B vitamins** (methylfolate, methylcobalamin), justified by MTHFR/COMT/MTRR gene variants identified with 10X's own genetic test.
- Hydration with mineral salts, hydrogen water, red light, and (in some appearances) methylene blue.

**Evidence:**
| Claim | Grade | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Morning sunlight helps circadian rhythm and sleep | **Moderate–strong** | Consistent with circadian science (also Huberman). |
| Grounding/earthing | **Weak** | A few small, mostly unblinded studies. There is no plausible mechanism for "absorbing electrons" and no RCT evidence on outcomes. |
| Breathwork raises oxygen and is anti-stress | **Mixed** | Slow, exhale-dominant breathing lowers arousal (moderate evidence). Hyperventilation-plus-hold protocols do **not** "oxygenate" you, since saturation is already about 97–99%. They lower CO2. Never do breath holds in water. |
| MTHFR variants cause most chronic disease, so everyone needs methylated B vitamins | **Contradicted / overstated** | About 10–15% of people are C677T homozygous. Major genetics bodies (ACMG) advise **against** routine MTHFR testing. B vitamins help only when there is a deficiency, such as low B12 (vegans, metformin users, older adults), which a cheap blood test can check. |
| Cold plunge | **Moderate (mood, alertness), weak for fat loss** | See the cold-exposure section below. |

**Red flags:** He sells the test and the supplements that the test "indicates," which is a direct conflict of interest. He makes sweeping disease claims without a medical license. Scientists such as Chris Masterjohn (PhD, nutrition) and Layne Norton have publicly corrected his biochemistry and physiology. He has a high-profile wellness-influencer platform but no peer-reviewed work.

**Usable for this plan:** Morning sunlight, slow breathing, time outdoors, and optional brief cold showers. **Exclude:** MTHFR testing or upsells and methylene blue. Check B12 and folate on a standard blood panel instead.

## 3. Max Gerson / Gerson Therapy

**Credentials:** German-born physician (1881–1959). The Gerson Institute (run by his family) promotes the regimen today, mainly for cancer.

**Protocol:** Organic, salt-free, almost fat-free vegetarian diet. Up to **13 glasses of fresh juice a day**. Supplements (potassium, Lugol's iodine, thyroid hormone, pancreatic enzymes, B12/liver injections in older versions). **Coffee enemas up to several times a day** to "detoxify the liver."

**Evidence:**
| Claim | Grade | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Treats cancer or "detoxifies" | **Contradicted** | Memorial Sloan Kettering says the toxin build-up has never been proven, nor the diet's ability to remove it. Cancer Research UK says the evidence does not support it and that it "can be very harmful." Australian cancer councils agree. |
| Coffee enemas | **Harmful** | They cause electrolyte imbalance, colitis, rectal burns and septicemia. Coffee enemas have been linked to at least 3 US deaths. |

**Red flags:** It has been blamed for deaths in patients who used it instead of standard care. The supplemental thyroid hormone and iodine are potentially dangerous.

**Usable for this plan:** Only the generic idea of "eat lots of vegetables." **Exclude everything specific to Gerson**, especially enemas, juice-only regimens and thyroid supplements.

## 4. Andrew Huberman

**Credentials:** PhD (UC Davis), tenured Professor of Neurobiology and Ophthalmology at Stanford. Expert in visual and neural circuits. He is **not** an MD, endocrinologist or nutrition researcher. He hosts the *Huberman Lab* podcast.

**Key protocols relevant to stress and cortisol:**
- **Morning outdoor light** within 30–60 min of waking: about 5–10 min on clear days and 15–30 min when overcast, without sunglasses or a window in the way. Avoid bright light from about 10 pm to 4 am.
- **Delay caffeine 90–120 min after waking** (theory: adenosine clearance and avoiding an afternoon crash). Cut caffeine about 8–10 h before bed.
- **Physiological sigh / cyclic sighing:** a double inhale through the nose, then a long exhale, for **5 min a day**.
- **NSDR / Yoga Nidra** for 10–20 min.
- **Deliberate cold:** about **11 min total per week**, split into 2–4 sessions (from the Søberg 2021 data). **Sauna:** about 57 min a week (same study).
- Exercise: Zone 2 cardio plus resistance training; sleep regularity is "foundational."
- **Sleep stack:** magnesium threonate (about **145 mg**) or bisglycinate (**200–400 mg**), apigenin **50 mg**, L-theanine **100–400 mg**, 30–60 min before bed. Avoid melatonin for routine use. Occasional GABA. (Doses from Huberman-site summaries.)
- **Ashwagandha:** described as useful for lowering cortisol and anxiety, taken in the evening, and cycled (for example 2 weeks on, 2 weeks off) because of thyroid/liver concerns. Typical trial dose is **300–600 mg/day of a root extract**.

**Evidence:**
| Claim | Grade | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Cyclic sighing reduces stress | **Moderate** | The Balban et al. 2023 RCT (*Cell Reports Medicine*, Huberman co-author, n≈108, 1 month) found cyclic sighing improved mood and lowered resting breathing rate more than mindfulness meditation did. It was a remote study with self-reported outcomes. |
| Morning light improves sleep and circadian timing | **Moderate–strong** | Circadian literature is consistent. |
| Delaying caffeine 90 min | **Weak** | It is a plausible idea but has no direct trial. Total dose and cut-off time matter more. |
| Ashwagandha lowers cortisol | **Moderate** | Meta-analyses of about 9–12 small RCTs show lower perceived stress and serum cortisol. Many trials are funded by manufacturers. Rare liver injury has been reported (NIH LiverTox), and it can raise thyroid hormone. Denmark banned it in 2023. |
| Magnesium, apigenin and theanine for sleep | **Weak–moderate** | Magnesium helps most when intake is low. Apigenin has almost no human trials. Theanine has small positive RCTs. |
| Cold 11 min/week | **Weak–moderate** | See the cold-exposure synthesis. |

**Red flags:** A 2024 *New York Magazine* exposé was mostly about his personal conduct. It also criticized him for heavy supplement sponsorships (AG1, Momentous) and for extrapolating from animal and mechanistic studies. He later disclosed using testosterone. Treat his protocol lists as hypotheses, not proven programs.

**Usable for this plan:** Morning light, cyclic sighing, NSDR, caffeine cut-off, sleep regularity, and optional magnesium, theanine or ashwagandha (ashwagandha with caveats).

## 5. Peter Attia

**Credentials:** MD (Stanford). Did general-surgery residency training at Johns Hopkins but did not complete it; he is not board-certified. He worked in consulting before founding a longevity practice. He wrote *Outlive* (2023) and hosts *The Drive*. He is among the more evidence-literate figures here and openly discusses uncertainty.

**Key protocols:**
- **Exercise is the #1 longevity drug.** Weekly targets: about **3–4 h of Zone 2** (conversational pace, roughly lactate threshold 1), **1–2 sessions of VO2max intervals** (for example 4×4 min hard / 4 min easy), **3–4 strength sessions**, plus stability work. VO2max and muscle strength are among the strongest predictors of mortality.
- **Protein:** about **1.6–2.2 g/kg/day**, often quoted as about **1 g per lb of target or lean body weight**, spread across meals.
- **Nutrition:** "caloric restriction, dietary restriction or time restriction, pick your lever." He has moved away from long fasts because of muscle loss and now favors adequate protein over extended fasting. He uses a CGM for glucose feedback.
- **Sleep:** treated as a pillar (7.5–8.5 h, consistent timing, cool dark room, no alcohol).
- **Visceral fat:** he emphasizes DEXA visceral-fat measurement, ApoB, insulin resistance (OGTT/insulin) and liver fat.
- **Supplements he reports taking:** EPA/DHA about **2–2.5 g EPA + ~1–1.5 g DHA** per day, vitamin D about **5,000 IU** (titrated to 40–60 ng/mL), magnesium in several forms (high total), plus prescription drugs: **rapamycin** about 5–8 mg weekly (intermittent), statin/PCSK9i, SGLT2i. He stopped metformin.

**Evidence:**
| Claim | Grade |
|---|---|
| Aerobic plus resistance training reduces visceral fat and mortality | **Strong.** Aerobic exercise has the most consistent visceral-fat effect (Ismail 2012 meta-analysis). Resistance training preserves lean mass during a deficit. |
| Higher protein during a deficit preserves muscle and satiety | **Strong–moderate** (1.2–1.6 g/kg is well-supported; benefits above 1.6 g/kg are smaller) |
| Omega-3 at about 2–4 g | **Moderate** for triglycerides; mixed for other outcomes |
| Rapamycin for human longevity | **Weak** (mouse data only; immunosuppressive; prescription only) |

**Red flags:** Few. He takes off-label rapamycin and SGLT2i and has a premium concierge business. His exercise volume (about 10 h/week) is high for beginners. Critics note his Zone 2 can be too intense for untrained people.

**Usable for this plan:** The exercise framework (scaled), the protein target, DEXA/waist tracking, sleep. Exclude off-label prescriptions.

## 6. Bryan Johnson (Blueprint)

**Credentials:** Tech entrepreneur (Braintree/Venmo), no medical or scientific training. Blueprint is run with a physician team and sells supplements, olive oil and food products.

**Protocol (reported, 2023–2025):**
- About **1,950–2,250 kcal/day**, mostly vegan ("Super Veggie," "Nutty Pudding," black lentils, extra-virgin olive oil about 1 tbsp several times a day). All eating is done by about **11 am** (window roughly 6–11 am), giving a **long gap of about 10 h before bed**.
- **Sleep is treated as the top priority**: fixed bedtime about 8:30 pm, dark cool room, no caffeine, no alcohol, wind-down routine, sleep tracking.
- Exercise about 1 h a day (strength, mobility, HIIT).
- **Over 100 pills/day** at various times (e.g., Ca-AKG, NAD precursors, spermidine, taurine, astaxanthin, zinc, multivitamins), plus prescription drugs (rapamycin until 2024), lasers, red light, hyperbaric oxygen, gene therapy abroad, plasma exchange, and **transfusion of his son's plasma** (later stopped as "no benefit").

**Evidence:**
| Element | Grade |
|---|---|
| Sleep regularity, no alcohol, early dinner, whole-food plant-heavy diet, daily exercise | **Strong–moderate** |
| Calorie restriction about 10–25% | **Moderate** for metabolic markers (CALERIE trial) |
| The 100-pill stack, gene therapy, plasma | **Weak / unproven**; n=1, no controls |
| Rapamycin | He **stopped** it in 2024 after skin infections, high glucose, lipid abnormalities and a higher resting heart rate. |

**Red flags:** It is an extreme, n=1 experiment with no controls. It is expensive and commercially motivated. Physicians and Scientific American have described this kind of longevity biohacking as a "dangerous experiment." It is also socially isolating, which itself works against stress management.

**Usable for this plan:** "Sleep is the #1 lever," an early last meal (3+ h before bed), and no alcohol. **Exclude:** supplement megastacks, plasma, off-label drugs, gene therapy.

## 7. Mark Hyman

**Credentials:** MD (family medicine). Founder of the UltraWellness Center and former head of strategy at the Cleveland Clinic Center for Functional Medicine. Co-founder of Function Health (lab testing). He has written many diet books (*Pegan*, *Food Fix*, *Young Forever*).

**Protocol:**
- "Pegan" diet: about 75% plants by volume, quality animal protein, healthy fats, low glycemic load, minimal sugar and flour. Limits dairy and gluten.
- Stress: meditation, breathing, sleep, "the UltraWellness" approach, magnesium, adaptogens, B-complex; "food is medicine."
- Lab-heavy approach (extensive testing), "detox," gut-health work.

**Evidence:** The whole-food, low-sugar, high-plant core is sound (**moderate–strong**). Avoiding gluten and dairy without a diagnosed intolerance is **weak**. "Detoxification principles" and functional-medicine test panels (e.g., food-sensitivity IgG tests) are **weak or unsupported**.

**Red flags:** He commercially sells tests, supplements and books. Dietitians have criticized him for unsupported claims about inflammation and longevity. Some functional-medicine tests are scientifically invalid.

**Usable for this plan:** A plant-heavy, protein-adequate, low-sugar whole-food plate. Skip expensive panels.

## 8. Rhonda Patrick

**Credentials:** PhD in biomedical science (UT/St. Jude), postdoctoral work at Children's Hospital Oakland Research Institute with Bruce Ames. Runs *FoundMyFitness*. Generally careful with citations, though she sometimes extrapolates from observational data.

**Protocol:**
- **Sauna:** relies on the Finnish Kuopio cohort, where 4–7 sessions a week at about **80 °C+ for 20 min** was associated with lower cardiovascular and all-cause mortality. She has stated that "hotter is not better."
- **Omega-3:** about **2 g EPA+DHA a day**, targeting an omega-3 index of **8% or higher**.
- **Vitamin D:** test it, target **40–60 ng/mL**, often about **2,000–5,000 IU** with K2.
- Magnesium, sulforaphane (broccoli sprouts), creatine (about **5–10 g**; she has discussed higher doses for cognition), and a multivitamin.
- Exercise: HIIT/Norwegian 4×4 plus strength. Time-restricted eating (about a 10 h window, finishing early). Has recently discussed microplastics.

**Evidence:**
| Claim | Grade |
|---|---|
| Sauna lowers mortality | **Moderate** (observational, plus short-term RCTs showing lower BP and better vascular function); for stress and sleep, weak–moderate |
| Omega-3 2 g | **Moderate** (triglycerides; small effects on mood and on cortisol reactivity in some studies) |
| Vitamin D if deficient | **Moderate**; little benefit if levels are already adequate (VITAL trial) |
| Creatine 3–5 g with training | **Strong** for strength and lean mass |

**Red flags:** Minor. She has supplement affiliate ties and sometimes overweights mechanistic or observational data.

**Usable for this plan:** Sauna 2–4×/week if available and medically safe; omega-3; vitamin D after a blood test; creatine 3–5 g with strength training.

## 9. Valter Longo (Fasting-Mimicking Diet)

**Credentials:** PhD, Professor of Gerontology and Biological Sciences at USC, director of the USC Longevity Institute. A legitimate researcher. **Conflict of interest:** founder and shareholder of L-Nutra (ProLon), though he says he donates his shares/profits to a foundation.

**Protocol:**
- **FMD:** 5 consecutive days a month of low-calorie, low-protein, plant-based food (about 1,100 kcal on day 1, about 700–800 kcal on days 2–5), repeated for **3 cycles**, then less often.
- **"Longevity Diet"** day to day: pescatarian, mostly plants, legumes and nuts, olive oil; **low protein (about 0.7–0.8 g/kg) under age 65, higher after 65**; eat within a 12 h window; 2 meals plus a snack if trying to lose weight.

**Evidence:**
| Claim | Grade |
|---|---|
| FMD 3 cycles lowers weight, waist, BP, IGF-1 and glucose in at-risk people | **Moderate** (a 2017 RCT, n≈100, crossover, with the largest effects in those with the highest risk; mouse visceral-fat data; mostly Longo's group and industry-linked) |
| FMD beats a plain calorie deficit of equal size | **Unproven** |
| Low protein under 65 | **Contested**; it conflicts with the muscle-preservation advice of Attia and others during fat loss |

**Red flags:** Industry ties. Not for people with diabetes on insulin or sulfonylureas (hypoglycemia), people with BMI under 18.5, pregnant women, or people with an eating-disorder history.

**Usable for this plan:** *Optional* 5-day FMD in month 2 or 3 for someone who finds it easier than daily restriction. Otherwise use a Mediterranean/pescatarian plate. Do not combine low protein with an aggressive deficit plus training.

## 10. Robert Lustig

**Credentials:** MD, emeritus Professor of Pediatric Endocrinology at UCSF, plus a master's in law. Author of *Fat Chance* and *Metabolical*.

**Protocol / thesis:**
- **Fructose (added sugar, especially in drinks) drives liver fat**, insulin resistance and visceral fat. The liver processes it somewhat like alcohol.
- "**Protect the liver, feed the gut**": eat **real food with fiber**, avoid ultra-processed food, cut sugar-sweetened beverages, eat whole fruit (not juice).
- Links chronic stress and cortisol to visceral fat and to "comfort food" (high-sugar, high-fat) seeking. Also discusses dopamine versus serotonin (*The Hacking of the American Mind*).

**Evidence:**
| Claim | Grade |
|---|---|
| Sugar-sweetened beverages raise visceral and liver fat | **Strong–moderate** (e.g., the Maersk 2012 RCT: 1 L/day of sucrose cola for 6 months raised liver and visceral fat; Framingham cohort data) |
| Fructose is uniquely "toxic" independent of calories | **Moderate / contested**. In isocaloric studies, effects are smaller. Critics (e.g., Sievenpiper) say the harm appears mainly with excess calories. |
| Ultra-processed food drives overeating | **Strong–moderate** (Hall 2019 NIH RCT: about +500 kcal/day on an ultra-processed diet) |
| Cortisol drives visceral fat deposition | **Moderate** (Cushing's physiology and stress-cortisol-waist association studies; the human causal chain is not fully pinned down) |

**Red flags:** Rhetoric ("sugar is poison") overstates the case. Otherwise his advice is mainstream.

**Usable for this plan:** **Zero liquid sugar**, whole fruit over juice, fiber of 30+ g/day, minimal ultra-processed food.

## 11. Jason Fung

**Credentials:** MD, nephrologist (Toronto). Author of *The Obesity Code* and *The Complete Guide to Fasting*. Co-founded IDM (Intensive Dietary Management).

**Protocol / thesis:**
- "Obesity is a hormonal (insulin) disorder, not a calorie disorder."
- **Intermittent fasting:** 16:8 daily, and/or **24 h fasts 2–3×/week**, and longer fasts under supervision. Low refined carbohydrates, no snacking, more fiber, vinegar.

**Evidence:**
| Claim | Grade |
|---|---|
| IF works for weight loss and type 2 diabetes | **Moderate**. It roughly matches daily calorie restriction head to head. |
| Fasting beats an equal calorie deficit (insulin model) | **Contradicted / not supported** (Liu 2022 NEJM: TRE 8am–4pm plus calorie restriction vs calorie restriction alone showed no difference in weight, visceral fat or liver fat at 12 months). Red Pen Reviews scored *The Obesity Code* 31% for scientific accuracy. |
| Low-carb helps glycemia in T2D | **Moderate–strong** for short-term HbA1c |

**Red flags:** He underplays calories. Long fasts are risky with diabetes medications (hypoglycemia), gout, a history of eating disorders, or pregnancy. There is risk of lean-mass loss without adequate protein and resistance training. **Relevant to cortisol:** prolonged fasting can *raise* cortisol and worsen sleep in some people.

**Usable for this plan:** A **10–12 h overnight fast / early time-restricted window** if it helps adherence. Avoid 24 h+ fasts for a stressed, under-slept person.

## 12. Sara Gottfried

**Credentials:** MD (Harvard), OB-GYN trained at UCSF, now in integrative/precision medicine (at Thomas Jefferson University's Marcus Institute). Author of *The Hormone Cure*, *Women, Food, and Hormones*, and *Brain Body Diet*.

**Protocol ("cortisol reset"):**
- Assess cortisol (she favors 4-point salivary or DUTCH urine testing). Classifies people as "high", "low" or "mixed" pattern.
- Lifestyle first: sleep, yoga, meditation, cut caffeine and alcohol, less intense HIIT when cortisol is "high," more restorative exercise.
- **Supplements she reports using:** fish oil about **2–4 g/day**, **phosphatidylserine 400 mg/day** (often at night for high evening cortisol), **rhodiola 200 mg once or twice daily** (a standardized extract), plus magnesium, vitamin C, B5, ashwagandha and holy basil.
- Diet: reduce sugar and refined carbohydrates, adequate protein, "detox" phases (removing alcohol, sugar, caffeine, gluten, dairy).

**Evidence:**
| Claim | Grade |
|---|---|
| Lifestyle-first cortisol management | **Moderate–strong** |
| "Adrenal fatigue" / "low cortisol" from stress | **Contradicted**. The Endocrine Society and a 2016 systematic review say "adrenal fatigue" is not a real diagnosis. |
| Salivary/DUTCH cortisol to guide supplements | **Weak**. Results vary heavily from day to day and are not validated for this use. |
| Phosphatidylserine lowers cortisol | **Weak–moderate**. Small studies show a blunted cortisol response to exercise or acute stress, often at 400–800 mg. |
| Rhodiola for fatigue and stress | **Weak–moderate** (small RCTs; the cortisol data are inconsistent) |

**Red flags:** Hormone-testing panels and the "adrenal" framing commercialize normal physiology. Otherwise her lifestyle advice is sensible and especially relevant for perimenopausal women.

**Usable for this plan:** Lifestyle-first sequencing, and the idea of dialling down intense training when sleep is poor or stress is high. Phosphatidylserine and rhodiola are optional add-ons with modest evidence. Don't buy cortisol test kits.

## 14. Other relevant figures (brief)

- **Satchin Panda (Salk, PhD):** circadian / time-restricted eating researcher. Recommends an **8–12 h eating window**, a consistent daily schedule, and finishing eating 2–3 h before bed. Evidence: **moderate** for sleep and metabolic markers, with the weight effects mostly explained by eating less.
- **Matthew Walker (UC Berkeley, PhD, *Why We Sleep*):** sleep is the base layer. Some claims in his book were shown to be exaggerated (e.g., Alexey Guzey's critique). The core message of 7–9 h with regular timing is **strong**.
- **Susanna Søberg (PhD, Copenhagen):** source of the "11 min cold / 57 min sauna per week" figure. It comes from a small, cross-sectional study of winter swimmers. **Weak** as a prescription.
- **Layne Norton (PhD, nutritional sciences):** an evidence-based counterweight. Energy balance, protein of 1.6–2.2 g/kg, resistance training and adherence beat specific diet tribes. He is a useful check on many of the claims above.
- **Rangan Chatterjee / Andrew Weil / Wim Hof:** Hof's breathing plus cold has **one notable RCT** (Kox 2014) showing a blunted inflammatory response to endotoxin. Risk: hyperventilation followed by breath holds in or near water has caused drownings.

## Cross-cutting evidence notes

**Cortisol and visceral fat:**
- Visceral adipose tissue has more glucocorticoid receptors and 11β-HSD1 activity. Chronic cortisol excess (Cushing's) produces central obesity. In non-clinical populations, chronic stress, short sleep, alcohol and high allostatic load all associate with a larger waist. **Practical lever:** you cannot measure or "detox" cortisol at home with any reliability. Instead, target the drivers: sleep, alcohol, overtraining, chronic under-recovery, social and psychological stressors.
- **Sleep:** In the Mayo Clinic RCT (Covassin 2022, *JACC*), 2 weeks of 4 h/night sleep raised visceral fat **11%** vs 9 h controls, with about **+300 kcal/day** extra intake, and the gain did not reverse with catch-up sleep. This is strong support for putting sleep first.

**Fat loss:**
- A **calorie deficit is required.** Visceral fat responds earlier and proportionally more than subcutaneous fat to weight loss and exercise.
- **Aerobic exercise** reduces visceral fat even without much weight loss (Ismail 2012 meta-analysis; dose-response at about 10+ MET-h/week, roughly 150–200 min of brisk walking). **Resistance training** preserves muscle and improves insulin sensitivity.
- **TRE** helps adherence but adds nothing on top of an equal deficit for visceral or liver fat (Liu 2022 NEJM).

**Cold and heat:**
- **Cold water immersion** meta-analysis (Cain 2025, *PLOS ONE*, 11 studies): a transient *rise* in inflammation, lower stress at 12 h only, possibly better sleep and quality of life, and no mood effect. It is not a fat-loss tool in practice (brown-fat calorie burn is small). Avoid within about 4–6 h after strength training if muscle growth is a goal (it blunts hypertrophy). Risky for people with heart disease or uncontrolled hypertension.
- **Sauna:** observational mortality data plus BP benefits. Contraindicated with unstable heart disease, alcohol, or pregnancy.

**Supplements (only those with some human evidence on stress or sleep):**
| Supplement | Typical evidence-based dose | Evidence | Key cautions |
|---|---|---|---|

## Synthesis

### Consensus practices (agreed on by almost all of them, and with good evidence)

| Practice | Who endorses | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Sleep 7–9 h, consistent wake time, dark/cool room, no late screens** | Huberman, Attia, Johnson, Patrick, Walker, Gottfried, Efferding, Hyman, Sinclair | Strong (Covassin 2022: sleep loss puts on visceral fat) |
| **Morning outdoor light, darkness at night** | Huberman, Brecka, Johnson, Panda, Gottfried | Moderate–strong |
| **Daily walking plus 150–300 min/week of aerobic (Zone 2) work** | Attia, Huberman, Patrick, Efferding, Johnson, Sinclair | Strong (visceral fat) |
| **Resistance training 2–4×/week** | Attia, Huberman, Patrick, Johnson, Efferding, Norton | Strong (muscle preservation, insulin sensitivity) |
| **Walk after meals** | Efferding, Attia, Huberman, Johnson | Moderate–strong |
| **A modest calorie deficit built on whole, minimally processed, plant-heavy food with adequate protein** | Everyone (they disagree on the proportions) | Strong |
| **Cut sugar-sweetened drinks, refined carbs and ultra-processed food** | Lustig, Fung, Hyman, Attia, Sinclair, Longo, Johnson | Strong |
| **Limit or avoid alcohol** | Attia, Huberman, Johnson, Gottfried, Hyman | Strong (sleep, visceral and liver fat) |
| **A daily down-regulation practice** (cyclic sighing 5 min, slow breathing, NSDR, meditation, nature) | Huberman, Gottfried, Hyman, Brecka | Moderate |
| **Some form of eating window / no late-night eating** (about 10–12 h window, last meal 2–3 h before bed) | Sinclair, Fung, Longo, Panda, Johnson, Patrick | Moderate (adherence and sleep; not superior for fat) |
| **Measure, don't guess:** waist circumference, weight trend, blood pressure, fasting glucose/HbA1c, lipids/ApoB, vitamin D, B12 | Attia, Hyman, Johnson, Patrick | Strong (as feedback) |

### Optional, reasonable add-ons (moderate evidence, low risk for most people)
- Sauna 2–4×/week at 15–20 min (if no heart disease).
- Cold showers or a cold plunge, 1–3 min, a few times a week, for alertness and mood. Not a fat-loss tool. Keep it away from strength sessions.
- Supplements: magnesium glycinate 200–400 mg in the evening; omega-3 1–2 g EPA+DHA; vitamin D only after testing; creatine 3–5 g; ashwagandha 300–600 mg for 8–12 weeks (with liver/thyroid caveats); L-theanine 100–200 mg.
- One FMD cycle (Longo) or 1–2 days/week of 14–16 h overnight fasting, only if it helps adherence and doesn't disrupt sleep.

### Fringe or unsafe: exclude from a sensible plan
| Exclude | Why |
|---|---|
| **Gerson therapy: coffee enemas, juice-only regimens, thyroid/iodine supplements** | No evidence; electrolyte disturbance, colitis, sepsis, deaths |
| **Gary Brecka's MTHFR testing / methylation upsell and methylene blue** | Genetics bodies advise against routine MTHFR testing; conflict of interest; methylene blue has serious interactions (serotonin syndrome with SSRIs) |
| **Grounding/earthing as therapy** | No credible mechanism or outcome data (harmless but a waste of time) |
| **Off-label prescription "longevity" drugs without indication: rapamycin, metformin, SGLT2i, testosterone, peptides** | Unproven for longevity in healthy people; real side effects (Johnson quit rapamycin after side effects); may blunt training adaptations |
| **Young-plasma transfusion, gene therapy, 100+ pill stacks** | No evidence, real risk, expensive |
| **NMN, NR, resveratrol for fat loss or cortisol** | No human evidence for these outcomes |
| **Extended water fasts (over 24–72 h) unsupervised; 24 h fasts several times a week for a stressed, short-sleeping person** | Can raise cortisol and cause muscle loss, hypoglycemia on medication, and eating-disorder risk; no advantage over an equal deficit |
| **"Adrenal fatigue" diagnosis, salivary/DUTCH cortisol-guided supplement protocols** | Not a valid diagnosis; tests are not validated for this |
| **Hyperventilation-plus-breath-hold breathwork in or near water, while driving, or with seizure/cardiac history** | Shallow-water blackout and drowning risk |
| **Ice baths over about 10 °C for long durations, or sauna, for people with heart disease, uncontrolled BP, or pregnancy, without clearance** | Cardiovascular risk |
| **Low-fiber, red-meat-centric Vertical Diet as a default for fat loss** | Weak evidence; conflicts with fiber and visceral-fat literature |

### One-line takeaway
The figures disagree on the gadgets and supplements, but they converge on **sleep, light, walking, lifting, whole-food protein-forward eating in a modest deficit, no liquid sugar or excess alcohol, and a daily calming practice**. That convergence lines up with the strongest evidence for lowering stress physiology and visceral fat in 3 months. Everything else is optional or should be dropped.
