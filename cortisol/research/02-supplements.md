# 02 — Supplements: Clinical Pharmacology Evidence Review

**Program:** 3-month cortisol-management and abdominal-fat-loss program  
**Subject:** adult, sex, age and medications unknown  
**Department:** Clinical Pharmacology  
**Date:** 2026-10-27

> **Read this first.** No supplement does more than a small fraction of what sleep, a calorie deficit, protein intake, resistance training and alcohol reduction do for visceral fat. Even the best-evidenced agents below give effects of about 1–3 cm of waist circumference or about 0.5–2 kg over 3 months. Supplements are an adjunct. Because the subject's medications, pregnancy status and liver, thyroid and kidney history are unknown, **everything in Tier 2 and Tier 3 needs a pharmacist or physician medication review first**. "Cortisol lowering" is only a goal when cortisol is actually dysregulated. Healthy cortisol has a steep morning peak, and flattening that peak is not the aim. Abnormally high cortisol (Cushing's) or low cortisol (adrenal insufficiency) is a medical diagnosis, not a supplement problem.

---

## 1. Evidence grading key

| Grade | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **A** | Consistent benefit in multiple RCTs and/or meta-analysis of RCTs |
| **B** | Moderate: some RCTs or meta-analyses with heterogeneity, small samples, industry funding or narrow populations |
| **C** | Weak: small or single trials, mechanistic or animal data, or mixed results |
| **D** | No meaningful human evidence, or null or negative evidence |

Grades are given separately for **(a) cortisol / perceived stress** and **(b) belly fat / body weight / metabolic health**.

## 3. Key safety signals (highlighted)

1. **Ashwagandha: liver injury and regulatory restrictions.** LiverTox (NIH) lists it as a probable cause of clinically apparent liver injury (category C). Injury is typically cholestatic, with jaundice and itch 2–12 weeks after starting, and usually resolves after stopping. **Denmark banned it in 2023** after a DTU risk assessment citing thyroid, sex-hormone and pregnancy (abortifacient) concerns. Other EU regulators have reviewed it. Thyrotoxicosis cases have been reported. **Rules:** check LFTs and TSH at baseline and repeat ALT and TSH at week 6–8; stop at the first sign of jaundice, dark urine, itch or right-upper-quadrant pain. Do not combine with other hepatotoxic supplements (green tea extract, high-dose niacin, kava, turmeric extracts).
2. **Green tea extract: hepatotoxicity.** EFSA found transaminases rise at ≥800 mg EGCG/day and could not define a safe dose. LiverTox lists dozens of acute hepatitis cases, including liver transplants. The risk is highest when taken fasted and from concentrated "fat-burner" blends. **This program uses no green tea extract; brewed tea is fine.**
3. **Berberine: a drug-like agent.** It inhibits CYP2D6, CYP2C9 and CYP3A4 in humans, causes additive **hypoglycemia** with metformin, sulfonylureas, insulin and GLP-1 RAs, has raised cyclosporine levels, and is contraindicated in pregnancy, breastfeeding and neonates. Prescriber approval is required.
4. **Serotonergic stacking.** Saffron, rhodiola, 5-HTP, St John's wort and high-dose methylfolate with SSRIs, SNRIs, triptans, tramadol or MAOIs can raise serotonin-syndrome risk. **St John's wort is a strong CYP3A4 inducer** (it defeats oral contraceptives and many other drugs); never add it.
5. **Thyroid.** Ashwagandha (raises T4) and lemon balm can affect thyroid function. Biotin (often in B-complex and "hair" products) **falsely skews TSH and T3/T4 immunoassays**; stop it 72 h before blood tests.
6. **Anticoagulants and antiplatelets.** Use caution with omega-3 (≥3 g), holy basil, resveratrol, saffron and chamomile/apigenin. **Vitamin K2 is contraindicated with warfarin.**
7. **Kidney disease.** Avoid magnesium, potassium-containing electrolytes and chromium. Creatine falsely lowers eGFR.
8. **Product quality.** Supplements are not pre-approved by the FDA. Buy only third-party-tested products (USP Verified, NSF Certified for Sport, Informed Choice). Weight-loss products are the category most often adulterated with sibutramine, phenolphthalein, laxatives and diuretics.
9. **Pregnancy, trying to conceive, or breastfeeding.** Use only the Tier 1 items (magnesium, omega-3, vitamin D at standard doses, psyllium) after OB approval. Avoid all botanicals.

## 4. Proposed tiered stack (3 months)

### Tier 1: foundation, low risk (start week 1)

| Item | Exact dose | When | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|

## 5. Not worth the money (or not worth the risk)

| Supplement | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **Green tea extract / EGCG "fat burners"** | About 1 kg effect, with a real hepatotoxicity risk. Drink the tea instead |
| **CLA** | About 0.5 kg effect, and the t10c12 isomer *worsens* insulin resistance in abdominally obese people |
| **Apple cider vinegar (gummies/pills)** | The flagship RCT was retracted in 2025. Gummies contain sugar; pills have injured the esophagus |
| **Chromium picolinate** | Effect is clinically insignificant (NIH ODS) |
| **NMN / NR** | No human body-composition benefit; expensive; regulatory questions |
| **Resveratrol** | Poor bioavailability; null human trials |
| **Phosphatidylserine** | Weak evidence from a single research group; costly at effective doses |
| **Raspberry ketones, garcinia cambogia (HCA), glucomannan pills, "cortisol blocker" blends (e.g. Relacore-type), 7-keto-DHEA, DHEA, "adrenal support" glandulars, bitter orange/synephrine, yohimbine** | Null or negative evidence, adulteration risk, stimulant cardiovascular risk, or hormonal effects. Garcinia is linked to liver failure (LiverTox). Glandulars may contain actual hormones |
| **Methylated B vitamins without a deficiency, and electrolyte powders without heavy sweating** | No benefit when not deficient |
| **Isolated apigenin** | Little human data; chamomile tea is a cheap substitute |

## 6. Laboratory monitoring: baseline and day 90

Draw blood fasting (8–12 h) **between 07:00 and 09:00**, after **no biotin for 72 h**, no strenuous exercise for 24 h and no alcohol for 48 h. Females: note cycle day (ideally days 2–5 for sex hormones).

| Test | Baseline | Day 90 | Rationale (one line) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **AM serum cortisol (08:00)** | ✓ | ✓ | Screens for extremes (Cushing's or adrenal insufficiency). A single value cannot show day-curve dysregulation |
| **4-point salivary cortisol** (wake, +30 min CAR, afternoon, bedtime) **or DUTCH Complete** | ✓ (optional) | ✓ (optional) | Shows the diurnal slope and evening cortisol, which is the real target. DUTCH adds cortisol metabolites. Not diagnostic and not insurance-covered; **late-night salivary cortisol or a 1 mg dexamethasone test are the medical screens if Cushing's is suspected** (central obesity, purple striae, easy bruising, proximal weakness, hypertension) |
| **Fasting glucose + fasting insulin → HOMA-IR** | ✓ | ✓ | Measures insulin resistance, the main driver of visceral fat. Determines berberine and inositol eligibility |
| **HbA1c** | ✓ | ✓ | 3-month average glucose; aligns exactly with the program window |
| **Lipid panel** (TC, LDL-C, HDL-C, TG; ± ApoB) | ✓ | ✓ | Triglyceride/HDL ratio tracks visceral fat; omega-3, psyllium and berberine all shift lipids. ApoB is the better risk marker |
| **hs-CRP** | ✓ | ✓ | Inflammation from visceral fat. Postpone the draw if the subject is acutely ill |
| **TSH + free T4 (± free T3; TPO antibodies at baseline)** | ✓ | ✓ | Hypothyroidism mimics fatigue and weight gain. **Safety monitor for ashwagandha.** TPO antibodies flag autoimmune thyroiditis |
| **25-OH vitamin D** | ✓ | ✓ | Sets the D3 dose and confirms the 30–50 ng/mL target without overshoot |
| **Ferritin + CBC (± iron/TIBC)** | ✓ | ✓ | Iron deficiency causes fatigue and mimics "adrenal fatigue". High ferritin may indicate fatty liver or inflammation |
| **CMP: ALT, AST, ALP, GGT, bilirubin, albumin, creatinine/eGFR, electrolytes** | ✓ | ✓ (**plus ALT/AST at week 6–8 if on ashwagandha or berberine**) | **Liver-safety monitoring for botanicals**, fatty liver screen (ALT), kidney function for magnesium and creatine (cystatin C if on creatine), potassium |
| **Magnesium (serum; RBC Mg optional)** | ✓ | — | Low magnesium supports supplementing; serum is insensitive |
| **B12 (± methylmalonic acid), folate** | ✓ | if low | Rules out deficiency (common with metformin or PPIs). Guides the B-complex decision |
| **Total testosterone + SHBG (± free T, estradiol); females: also LH/FSH if irregular cycles** | ✓ | ✓ | Low T in men drives visceral fat (and is lowered by it). High androgens or low SHBG in women suggest PCOS and insulin resistance. Ashwagandha can raise testosterone |
| **Uric acid** | ✓ | ✓ | Metabolic syndrome marker; rises with rapid weight loss |
| **Optional: GGT, ApoB, Lp(a) (once), homocysteine, urinary albumin/creatinine ratio** | ✓ | — | Further cardiometabolic risk stratification |

**Non-blood measurements (baseline, monthly, day 90):** waist circumference at the navel and at the iliac crest, waist-to-height ratio (target <0.5), weight, BP, resting HR, HRV trend, PSS-10 stress questionnaire, PSQI sleep score. **Optional: DEXA scan** (visceral adipose tissue in grams) at baseline and day 90.
