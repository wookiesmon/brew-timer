# 03 — Lifestyle Medicine: Cortisol Management + Belly (Visceral) Fat Loss, 12 Weeks

*Department: Lifestyle Medicine. Audience: one adult (possibly Korean, so Korean food options are included). This is educational material, not a diagnosis. Anyone with medical conditions, medications (especially steroids, insulin/sulfonylureas, blood-pressure or heart drugs), pregnancy, or an eating-disorder history should review this with a clinician first.*

---

## 0. Executive summary

- **Visceral fat responds best to an energy deficit plus regular exercise.** Exercise reduces visceral fat in a dose-dependent way, even with little change on the scale (Recchia 2023 BJSM; Chen 2024 Obes Rev network meta-analysis of 84 RCTs). The main contributors are moderate-or-harder aerobic work and HIIT, with resistance training added to protect muscle.
- **Lifestyle can't "target" cortisol directly**, but it can lower the load on the stress system. The best-supported levers are enough regular sleep, regular exercise without overtraining, slow breathing, mindfulness or yoga, time in nature, and social contact. Effects on measured cortisol are small to moderate and inconsistent, and they are clearest in people who are stressed or at risk. **Expect better perceived stress, HRV, sleep, and waist size. Do not expect a lab cortisol value to "normalise".**
- **Nutrition:** a Mediterranean-style or balanced Korean pattern. Aim for protein of about 1.2–1.6 g/kg/day, fiber of 25–38 g/day or more (with soluble fiber emphasised), little sugar-sweetened drink or fructose, little ultra-processed food or alcohol, and no late-night eating. Time-restricted eating (TRE) is optional. Choose a gentle 10–12 h window over long fasts.
- **12-week realistic target:** lose 0.25–1% of body weight per week, about 3–8% in total. Reduce waist by about 3–8 cm. Move waist-to-height ratio toward < 0.5. Lower PSS-10 by 3 or more points. Raise average HRV and lower resting heart rate.

Evidence grades used throughout: **A** = consistent meta-analyses or RCTs; **B** = some RCTs or meta-analyses with limitations; **C** = small or preliminary trials, observational, or mechanistic evidence only; **D** = no credible evidence, or the risk outweighs the benefit.

## 1. Nutrition

### 1.1 Core targets

| Target | Recommendation | Evidence / rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Energy | Moderate deficit of about 300–500 kcal/day. No crash diets. | Visceral fat drops preferentially with weight loss. Fasting-level restriction raises cortisol acutely, while milder calorie restriction does not (Nakamura 2016 meta-analysis). |
| Protein | **1.2–1.6 g/kg body weight/day**, spread as 25–40 g per meal | Higher protein during weight loss preserves lean mass and increases fat-mass loss (Kim 2016 meta-analysis, 20 RCTs in older adults). Benefit above ~1.0–1.3 g/kg is debated (Backx 2016 RCT), so 1.2–1.6 is a sensible range, not a magic number. With kidney disease, ask a doctor first. |
| Fiber | **25–38 g/day** total. Aim for ~10 g/day of **soluble** fiber (oats, barley, legumes, beans, apples, psyllium). | In the IRAS Family Study (CT-measured), each +10 g/day of soluble fiber was associated with ~3.7% less visceral-fat gain over 5 years. That is observational. |
| Pattern | Mediterranean-style: vegetables, legumes, whole grains, fish, olive oil, nuts, fermented foods | Strongest overall cardiometabolic evidence. Compatible with the traditional Korean "bap + many banchan" pattern if it is low in sodium and high in variety. |
| Added sugar / fructose | Under ~25 g/day of added sugar. **No sugar-sweetened drinks or fruit juice.** | In an RCT, fructose-sweetened drinks increased visceral fat compared with glucose drinks (Stanhope 2009 JCI). |
| Ultra-processed food (UPF) | Keep to a minimum | In an inpatient RCT, people on a UPF diet ate ~500 kcal/day more and gained weight (Hall 2019 Cell Metab). |
| Alcohol | Ideally none. At most 1 drink, on 2 or fewer days per week. **Never within 3 h of bed.** | Adds calories, disinhibits eating, fragments sleep, and lowers HRV overnight. Heavy drinking is linked with central fat and raised cortisol. |
| Caffeine | 1–3 cups of coffee/tea before **~noon** (cutoff ~8–10 h before bed). Up to 400 mg/day for most adults. | See 1.3. |
| Late-night eating | Last meal **≥ 3 h before bed**. Kitchen closes around 20:00. | Late eating is linked to worse glucose tolerance and sleep. It also aligns with circadian findings. |
| Sodium (esp. Korean diet) | Under 2,000 mg/day of sodium (WHO), which is about 5 g of salt | In KNHANES, the top sodium sources are noodles/dumplings, **kimchi, and soups/stews**. |

### 1.2 Time-restricted eating (TRE) and fasting

- **Evidence:** A 2025 meta-analysis of 13 RCTs (612 women with overweight or obesity) found TRE lowered body weight and fasting insulin, but **did not significantly reduce visceral fat** compared with controls. Most benefit comes from eating less overall. A 2022 trial in women found TRE plus HIIT reduced visceral fat more than either alone.
- **Does fasting raise cortisol?** A meta-analysis (Nakamura et al. 2016, *Stress*) found **true fasting acutely raises serum cortisol**, while low-calorie and very-low-calorie diets did not significantly do so. Cortisol rises early in calorie restriction and returns to baseline after several weeks. Ramadan data suggest a shifted rhythm (lower morning cortisol) rather than a uniform increase.
- **Practical recommendation:** use a gentle **10–12 h daytime eating window** (for example 08:00–19:00/20:00), which mainly serves to stop late-night eating. **Avoid fasts of 16:8 or longer, and 24 h or longer, in this program** if any of these apply: high perceived stress, poor sleep, female with menstrual irregularity, history of disordered eating, diabetes on medication, or pregnancy. Do not skip breakfast and then do hard training while fasted.

### 1.3 Caffeine and the cortisol awakening response (CAR)

- Caffeine raises cortisol acutely, but **habitual users develop substantial tolerance**. In Lovallo's lab, 5 days at 300–600 mg/day abolished the cortisol rise to a morning dose in many participants.
- Controlled studies have not shown that coffee changes the CAR itself. The popular rule to "wait 90 minutes for coffee" is **not evidence-based**. It is harmless if it helps you, though.
- **What matters more:** a cutoff around noon or 8–10 h before bed, protecting sleep, and keeping total intake moderate. Anxious people, or those with palpitations or reflux, should cut down or switch to green tea, which has less caffeine plus L-theanine.

### 1.4 Eat more / eat less

**Eat more:** non-starchy vegetables (half the plate), legumes, beans, tofu, natto, cheonggukjang, oats, barley, mixed-grain rice (japgokbap, ≥ 50% non-white grains), berries, apples, citrus, fatty fish (mackerel/godeungeo, salmon, sardines), eggs, Greek yogurt, kefir, nuts and seeds, perilla, extra-virgin olive oil, kimchi and other fermented vegetables (low-salt, small portions), seaweed (miyeok, gim without added oil and salt), mushrooms, green tea, water.

**Eat less / avoid:** sugary drinks, juice, sweetened coffee drinks (dalgona, syrup lattes), pastries, white bread, large mounds of white rice, instant ramyeon, tteokbokki, fried chicken (chimaek is a double hit with alcohol), processed meats (spam, sausage, bacon), soju and beer, late-night delivery food (yasik), salty broths drunk to the bottom, candy, ultra-processed snacks.

A "balanced Korean diet" (rice and kimchi plus a variety of other foods) was associated with **lower** metabolic syndrome in women. An "unbalanced" pattern high in carbohydrate and sodium was associated with **higher** risk (KNHANES 2007–08).

### 1.6 Sample day (≈ 1,700–2,000 kcal, ~120 g protein, ~35 g fiber; scale for body size)

- **07:30 Breakfast:** 2–3 eggs (or gyeran-jjim) + ½ bowl japgokbap or 40 g oats + kimchi (small) + gim + spinach namul. Or Greek yogurt (200 g) + berries + 1 Tbsp chia + walnuts. Coffee or green tea.
- **12:30 Lunch:** Grilled mackerel or chicken breast (120–150 g) + ½–⅔ bowl japgokbap + 2–3 vegetable banchan + doenjang-guk (eat the solids, little broth). **10–15 min walk after.**
- **15:30 Snack (optional):** an apple + a handful of almonds, or soy milk (unsweetened) + boiled egg.
- **18:30 Dinner (≥ 3 h before bed):** Tofu-vegetable jjigae or bibimbap with extra vegetables, tofu or beef, and ½ portion rice. Or salmon + big salad with olive oil + lentils. **10-min walk after.**
- **Evening:** boricha / chamomile. Kitchen closed.

### 1.7 Weekly grocery list (1 person)

- **Protein:** eggs (15–20), tofu (3–4 blocks), chicken breast/thigh (1 kg), mackerel/salmon/sardines (3–4 portions; canned is fine), lean beef or pork (400 g), Greek yogurt or plain yogurt (1 kg), unsweetened soy milk, cheonggukjang or natto.
- **Carbohydrate/fiber:** japgok mix (brown rice, barley, black beans), rolled oats, sweet potatoes, chickpeas/lentils (canned or dry), whole-grain bread (optional).
- **Vegetables:** spinach, kongnamul (bean sprouts), napa cabbage, zucchini (aehobak), mushrooms (pyogo/enoki), broccoli, lettuce and perilla leaves for ssam, onions, garlic, green onion, peppers, cucumber, frozen mixed vegetables, miyeok (dried seaweed), gim (unsalted if possible).
- **Fruit:** berries (frozen is fine), apples, citrus, kiwi.
- **Fats:** extra-virgin olive oil, sesame or perilla oil (small amounts), walnuts/almonds, chia or ground flax.
- **Fermented/condiments:** kimchi (low-sodium if available), doenjang, gochujang (sparingly, as it is sugar plus salt), vinegar, low-sodium soy sauce.
- **Drinks:** coffee beans, green tea, boricha/oksusu-cha, chamomile.
- **Optional:** psyllium husk (5 g/day adds about 4 g soluble fiber).

## 2. Exercise

### 2.1 What reduces visceral fat

- **Chen et al. 2024 (Obesity Reviews), network meta-analysis of 84 RCTs (n = 4,836):** aerobic exercise of at least moderate intensity, HIIT, combined aerobic + resistance, and resistance training all reduced visceral fat. Aerobic and HIIT ranked highest.
- **Recchia et al. 2023 (BJSM), 40 RCTs, n = 2,190:** exercise reduced visceral fat **in a dose-dependent way**, which caloric restriction did not. More weekly exercise volume means more visceral fat lost, even without large weight change.
- An earlier network meta-analysis (2021, *IJO*) found HIIT and moderate-or-harder aerobic exercise effective, with resistance alone not significant. **Conclusion:** cardio and HIIT drive visceral fat loss, and resistance training protects muscle and metabolic health. **Do both.**
- **Weekly dose:** WHO advises 150–300 min of moderate activity or 75–150 min vigorous, **plus 2 or more days of strength training**. For visceral fat, aim for the upper end, about 200–300 min/week in total.

### 2.2 Weekly template (weeks 1–4 build; 5–12 progress)

| Day | Session | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | **Strength A** (full body: squat/goblet squat, push-up/bench, row, hip hinge/RDL, plank) 2–3 sets × 8–12 reps, RPE 7 | 40–45 min |
| Tue | **Zone 2** brisk walk/cycle/jog (can talk in full sentences; ~60–70% HRmax) | 40–50 min |
| Wed | **Strength B** (lunge/split squat, overhead press, lat pulldown/band pull, glute bridge, carries) | 40–45 min |
| Thu | **HIIT** (from week 3): warm up 10 min, then 4–6 × 1 min hard / 2 min easy, cool down. Or Zone 2 if sleep or stress is poor. | 25–30 min |
| Fri | **Strength C or Zone 2** + mobility/yoga | 40 min |
| Sat | **Long Zone 2** — hiking in a forest or park (combines with nature exposure) | 60–90 min |
| Sun | **Rest / gentle yoga / walk** | — |

Progression: each week add one rep or a small load to strength work. Add 5–10 min to Zone 2 sessions until you reach about 250 min/week of cardio. Every 4th week (weeks 4 and 8), deload by cutting volume by about 40%.

### 2.3 Steps and walking after meals

- **Daily steps:** Paluch 2022 (Lancet Public Health; 15 cohorts, n = 47,471) found mortality risk falls until about **8,000–10,000 steps/day under age 60** and about **6,000–8,000 at 60 or older**, then plateaus. **Target: 8,000–10,000/day.** Raise it by about 1,000/day each week from your baseline.
- **Post-meal walks:** In a meta-analysis (Buffey 2022, *Sports Med*, 7 studies), light walking breaks lowered glucose about 17% compared with prolonged sitting, and standing lowered it about 9.5%. Even **2–5 min** of walking after eating helps. **Target: 10–15 min after lunch and dinner.**

### 2.4 Overtraining and cortisol

- Cortisol rises with exercise **intensity and duration**. Each session's spike is normal and adaptive. **Chronic** high volume with poor recovery shows up as a falling testosterone:cortisol ratio, and sometimes as a blunted cortisol response (overreaching or overtraining). It is a marker of training strain, with no validated cutoff.
- **Rules for a stressed person:** HIIT no more than 2× per week. Keep hard sessions to 60 min or less. Take at least 1 full rest day. Deload every 4th week. Do not pair hard training with long fasts or < 6 h sleep.
- **Downshift signals** (swap that day's session for Zone 2 or a walk): HRV more than ~1 SD below your 7-day baseline for 2 or more days, resting HR 5 or more bpm above baseline, poor sleep, persistent soreness, irritability, or performance falling for 2 or more weeks.

## 3. Sleep and circadian rhythm

| Practice | Target | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Duration | **7–9 h/night** in bed long enough to achieve it | AASM/SRS consensus: 7 h or more for adults (Watson 2015). In Nedeltcheva 2010 (Ann Intern Med), 5.5 h vs 8.5 h of sleep during the same diet cut fat loss from 1.4 kg to 0.6 kg and increased lean-mass loss by 60%. Sleep loss also raises evening cortisol and hunger. |
| Consistency | Same wake time ±30 min, **including weekends** | Sleep regularity independently predicts cardiometabolic outcomes. A consistent wake time anchors the cortisol awakening response. |
| Morning light | Outdoors **10–30 min within 1 h of waking**. Longer if overcast. | Daylight is the strongest zeitgeber. Brown et al. 2022 (PLOS Biology) consensus: daytime ≥ 250 lux melanopic EDI at the eye. Indoor light rarely reaches this, but outdoor light does easily. |
| Evening light | **Dim lights (≤ 10 lux melanopic EDI) from 3 h before bed.** Warm lamps below eye level. Night mode on screens. | Brown 2022. Bright evening light suppresses melatonin and delays the clock. |
| Bedroom | Dark (blackout or eye mask), **cool (~17–20 °C)**, quiet (earplugs or white noise), phone outside the bedroom or face down on Do Not Disturb | Standard CBT-I sleep hygiene |
| Caffeine/alcohol | Caffeine before about noon. No alcohol within 3 h of bed. | |
| Wind-down | 30–60 min: shower, reading, breathing, journaling | |
| Insomnia | If problems last 3 months or more, **CBT-I** is first-line, ahead of sleeping pills | ACP guideline |
| Snoring/gasping, daytime sleepiness | **Get screened for sleep apnea.** It is common with central obesity and raises cortisol and BP. | |

## 4. Stress practices (cortisol, HRV, perceived stress)

| Practice | Dose | Evidence | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cyclic physiological sigh** (2 inhales through the nose, the second a top-up, then a long slow exhale through the mouth) | **5 min/day** + 1–3 sighs as needed in acute stress | Balban 2023 (Cell Reports Medicine) remote RCT, n ≈ 108, 1 month: cyclic sighing improved positive mood and lowered respiratory rate **more than mindfulness meditation**. Small, single-lab, and co-authored by a promoter of the technique. | B |
| **Resonance / slow breathing ~5.5–6 breaths/min** (e.g., inhale 4–5 s, exhale 5–6 s) | 10–20 min/day. Optionally with an HRV biofeedback app or device. | Goessl 2017 meta-analysis (24 studies, n = 484): HRV biofeedback reduced stress and anxiety, g = 0.83 vs control. Raises HRV acutely and robustly. | A–B |
| **Box breathing** (4-4-4-4) | 5 min | Improved mood in Balban 2023, but less than cyclic sighing | B– |
| **Mindfulness / MBSR** | 10–20 min/day or an 8-week MBSR course | Koncz 2021 meta-analysis: medium cortisol reduction in blood samples in **at-risk** groups; not significant in saliva overall. Pascoe 2017 meta-analyses: lower cortisol, BP, and HR. Strong evidence for perceived stress, anxiety, and depression. | A (perceived stress) / B (cortisol) |
| **Yoga** | 2–3 × 45–60 min/week, or 15 min daily | Pascoe 2017 (Psychoneuroendocrinology, 42 studies): asana-based yoga reduced evening and waking cortisol, BP, and HR compared with active controls | B |
| **Nature exposure / forest bathing** | 120 min/week or more in green space (e.g., weekend forest hike) | Antonelli 2019 meta-analysis: lower cortisol in forest vs urban/control settings. White 2019 (Sci Rep): 120 min/week or more associated with better health and wellbeing. | B |
| **Social connection** | Real contact on most days. A weekly scheduled activity with friends or a group. | Holt-Lunstad 2010 meta-analysis (148 studies): strong social relationships carry ~50% higher odds of survival, an effect comparable to smoking cessation. Social support buffers the cortisol stress response in lab studies. | A (health outcomes) |
| **Expressive writing / journaling** | 15–20 min, 3–4 sessions on stressors. Or a daily 3-line gratitude/"worry dump" before bed. | Frattaroli 2006 (146 studies): small but significant benefit. Some meta-analyses are null. Best with 3 or more sessions of 15 min or more, written in private. | B– |
| **Music** | 20–30 min of calming music, e.g., during the wind-down | de Witte 2020 (104 RCTs): reduced physiological stress (d = 0.38) and psychological stress (d = 0.55). Hormone outcomes d ≈ 0.35. | B |

| **Yoga** | **B** | See section 4 | Modify for injuries, glaucoma (inversions), pregnancy |

## 6. When to see a doctor — Cushing's red flags and "adrenal fatigue"

### 6.1 Cushing's syndrome (true hypercortisolism)
Ordinary belly fat plus stress is **not** Cushing's. Cushing's is rare, comes from steroid medicines, a pituitary or adrenal tumour, or ectopic ACTH, and needs proper testing: late-night salivary cortisol, 24-h urinary free cortisol, or a 1 mg dexamethasone suppression test.

**The Endocrine Society's most discriminating features** (Nieman 2008 guideline):
- **Easy bruising** without trauma
- **Facial plethora** (red, round "moon" face)
- **Proximal muscle weakness**: hard to rise from a chair or climb stairs without using your arms
- **Wide (> 1 cm) reddish-purple striae** on the abdomen, thighs, or breasts
- (In children: weight gain with slowing height growth)

**Other features that, in combination, should prompt a doctor visit:** rapid central weight gain with thin arms and legs, dorsocervical fat pad ("buffalo hump") or supraclavicular fullness, new or worsening hypertension or diabetes **at a young age** or resistant to treatment, osteoporosis or fractures at a young age, thin fragile skin and poor wound healing, irregular or absent periods, new hirsutism or acne, low libido, frequent infections, kidney stones, severe mood or cognitive change, and an incidentally found adrenal mass.

**Also see a doctor** for: any steroid use (pills, inhalers, injections, creams, including "herbal" products adulterated with steroids). Weight gain despite genuinely following the program for 8 or more weeks. Heavy snoring or witnessed apneas. Persistent depression or anxiety, or thoughts of self-harm (urgent). Symptoms of thyroid disease. Unexplained fatigue (check anaemia, thyroid, glucose, vitamin D, B12). Suspected PCOS or menopause transition.

### 6.2 Why "adrenal fatigue" is not a diagnosis
- The **Endocrine Society** states that no scientific proof exists that adrenal fatigue is a true medical condition. A systematic review (Cadegiani & Kater 2016) found no substantiation for it.
- The theory that chronic stress "exhausts" the adrenals has not held up. In real **adrenal insufficiency** (Addison's), patients lose weight, become hypotensive and darkly pigmented, crave salt, and risk adrenal crisis. That is diagnosed with a morning cortisol and an ACTH stimulation test, not saliva panels.
- **Risks of the label:** the real cause (sleep apnea, depression, thyroid disease, anaemia, medication effects) gets missed, and people buy unregulated "adrenal support" supplements, some of which contain thyroid or steroid hormone. **Do not buy commercial "4-point salivary cortisol / adrenal fatigue" panels.**

## 7. Measuring progress over 12 weeks

### 7.1 Metrics, method, and targets

| Metric | How | Frequency | Week-12 target |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Waist circumference** | Morning, fasted, after the toilet. Stand relaxed, feet together. Tape horizontal, **midway between the lowest rib and the top of the iliac crest** (WHO). Measure at the end of a normal exhale, snug but not compressing. Take 2 readings and average them. (Navel level is an acceptable alternative if used consistently.) | Weekly (same day) | **−3 to −8 cm** |
| **Waist-to-height ratio** | Waist ÷ height (same units) | Weekly | **< 0.5** (NICE 2022: 0.5–0.59 increased risk; ≥ 0.6 high risk), or at least −0.03 from baseline |
| **Weight** | Morning, fasted, after the toilet. Use the **7-day average**. | Daily or ≥ 3×/week | −0.25 to −1%/week, **about −3 to −8% total** |
| **Resting HR** | Wearable overnight value, or 1 min manual count on waking | Daily (use the weekly average) | −2 to −5 bpm from baseline |
| **HRV** (rMSSD, overnight or morning; same device) | Wearable. Compare only with your own baseline. | Daily → weekly average | Weekly mean ↑ (~+5–10%). Fewer days below baseline. |
| **Sleep** | Wearable sleep score + sleep diary (bed/wake time, awakenings) | Daily | ≥ 7 h on ≥ 5 nights/week. Wake time SD ≤ 30 min. |
| **Perceived stress (PSS-10)** | 10-item Perceived Stress Scale (Cohen), 0–40. 0–13 low, 14–26 moderate, 27–40 high. | Weeks 0, 4, 8, 12 | **≥ 3–5 point drop** (or into the low band) |
| **Energy / mood** | 1–10 daily rating. Optional WHO-5 wellbeing index. | Daily / monthly | Average +2 points |
| **Photos** | Front, side, back. Same light, clothes, time, and distance. | Every 2 weeks | Visual change |
| **Fitness** | Push-ups to form failure, 30-s sit-to-stand, 1.5 km walk time or Zone 2 pace at a fixed HR | Weeks 0, 6, 12 | Improvement in all |
| **Adherence** | Steps, exercise minutes, protein/fiber hit rate | Daily | ≥ 80% of days |
| **Labs** (via doctor) | Fasting glucose, HbA1c, lipid panel, ALT/AST (fatty liver), TSH, BP. Optional fasting insulin/HOMA-IR. **Cortisol tests only if Cushing's red flags are present.** Optional body composition (DEXA or BIA, which can estimate visceral fat). | Baseline and week 12 | Improved TG, HDL, glucose, ALT, BP |

### 7.2 Realistic rate of fat loss
- A safe, sustainable pace is **0.5–1 kg/week** (about 0.5–1% of body weight; CDC: 1–2 lb/week). Expect faster loss in weeks 1–2 (glycogen and water), then slower. Over 12 weeks that is roughly **4–8 kg for most people** with overweight. Leaner people will lose less.
- **Visceral fat is lost preferentially:** 5–10% weight loss can cut visceral fat by ~15–30%. Exercise lowers visceral fat even with little weight change, which is why **waist is the primary metric, not the scale**.
- Expect plateaus, menstrual-cycle water swings (compare the same cycle phase), and noise from salty meals. Judge trends over 2–4 weeks.

## 8. Daily routine template (adjust to your wake time)

| Time | Action |
|---|---|
